Bolt privacy policy
Effective October 4, 2026

Who operates Bolt

Bolt is an early-access Windows file-transfer application operated by Varun Gotmare. For privacy questions or account-deletion requests, contact varunpgotmare@gmail.com. This policy covers the Bolt desktop application and this information website.

Nearby sharing

No account is needed for nearby sharing. When you choose Visible, Bolt broadcasts your device name, a device identifier, connection port, and certificate fingerprint to other devices on the local network. Nearby peers also see your network address. Your email is not included in that local broadcast. Device names are self-reported, not verified identities. Hidden mode stops announcements and new incoming nearby transfers.

Sign-in and internet sharing

If you choose Google sign-in, Google provides basic identity information to Supabase, Bolt’s authentication provider. This can include your account identifier, email, name, and profile image. Bolt uses your account identity and email to authenticate you and find devices for internet sharing. Bolt does not request access to Gmail messages, Google Drive files, contacts, or calendars. Email-code sign-in is an alternative where delivery is available.

The backend stores account records, device names and identifiers, certificate fingerprints, visibility, last-seen timestamps, connection-negotiation messages, and rate-limit counters. Negotiation messages can include network addresses and connection details. A signed-in person who knows your exact email can find your Visible, recently online devices. A recipient sees the sender’s identity and the files offered before deciding whether to accept.

File contents

Files and filenames are not sent to Bolt’s application API or stored in its database. They travel between devices over encrypted transfer connections. When direct internet connectivity fails, a TURN relay can forward encrypted traffic; Bolt does not use the relay as offline file storage. Relays handle network addresses and usage information required to forward traffic. The sender and recipient can access the files, and recipients can retain or share what they receive. Encryption does not make a compromised device safe.

Service providers

Bolt uses Supabase for authentication, device metadata and signaling; Google for optional Google sign-in; Resend for configured email-code delivery; Metered for the configured test relay; and GitHub for this website, release downloads and update checks. These providers may process IP addresses, request metadata, security logs, and service usage under their own policies. Data may be processed in countries other than yours. Bolt’s website contains no added analytics, advertising, tracking scripts or sign-in forms; GitHub may collect hosting access information.

Local storage and retention

Bolt stores preferences, device identity material and an encrypted session on your Windows account. Sign-in tokens are protected using Windows account encryption (DPAPI). Sign-out clears the local sign-in session; uninstalling may leave preferences and personal data on disk. Received files remain in the location you select until you delete them.

Online availability expires after about 90 seconds without a heartbeat. During subsequent heartbeat processing, the backend removes signaling records older than three minutes and inactive device records older than seven days. This cleanup is activity-triggered, not a guarantee of deletion at an exact time. Account and rate-limit records have no automatic deletion schedule in the current build. Provider logs and backups follow provider retention settings.

Your choices and requests

You can use nearby sharing without signing in, change your device name, select Hidden, decline transfers, sign out, quit Bolt, or disable automatic update checks. To request account/data deletion or ask about your data, email the contact above from your account address. Account deletion is currently a manual process, not an in-app button. Deletion cannot remove copies of files already held by recipients, and provider backups or required security records may persist according to their policies. You can also remove Bolt’s Google access in your Google account settings.

Updates to this policy

Material changes to Bolt’s data handling will be reflected here with an updated date. Review this policy before using new features. Contact the operator if anything is unclear.
